# Literature Review

## Scope

Use approximately 20–30 high-quality, relevant papers as the normal evidence target. The primary time window is the most recent five years, with older seminal work added when necessary.

Stop adding papers when the main method families, contradictory findings, and important transferability questions are adequately covered. Do not dilute the evidence set just to hit 30.

## Quality criteria

Assess papers using:

- direct relevance;
- peer-review rigor;
- journal/conference quality;
- experimental design;
- strength of baselines;
- ablation quality;
- reproducibility;
- code availability;
- applicability to the current task/model/metric.

Impact factor above roughly 10 may be a journal-quality signal where meaningful, but do not exclude rigorous top-tier conference work merely because conference proceedings do not have journal IF.

## Extraction template

For each paper used in decision-making record:

- citation;
- year and venue;
- research problem;
- task and dataset;
- architecture/model family;
- baseline;
- proposed modification;
- reported effect size on relevant metrics;
- ablation evidence;
- compute cost if reported;
- limitations;
- official code availability;
- direct relevance to this project;
- evidence-transferability level.

## Do not infer from title alone

Read enough of the methods and experiments to verify that the paper actually supports the claim for which it is cited.
